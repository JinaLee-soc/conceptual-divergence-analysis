# Revision memo: responses on the concepts of novelty and consensus

## Working draft — internal notes before resubmission

Reviewer A's central objection is that the manuscript "redefines novelty
until it is unrecognizable." I want to resist the framing while conceding
the observation. The manuscript does not redefine novelty; it relocates it.
The standard vocabulary locates novelty inside ideas — in recombination, in
distance from prior art, in disruption scores. We locate novelty in the
audience's repair work, and everything the standard vocabulary measures
reappears in our account as advertising: the materials a claiming group
prepares so that repair, if it happens, happens on favorable terms. The
revision should say this in one sentence early: novelty is not what a paper
has, it is what an audience does.

Concretely, I will add a boxed contrast. Under the property view, novelty
exists at submission time and reviewers detect it. Under the repair view,
novelty does not exist at submission time; reviewers are the first audience
whose repair work can begin to ratify it, which is why identical manuscripts
meet different fates in ways that mortify the property view and are
predicted by ours. The Halvorsen material carries the burden here: the pulse
episode shows ratified novelty emerging weeks after the signal, and the
recalibration episode shows a group deliberately declining to advertise
novelty to conserve the community's repair budget.

Reviewer B asks whether our consensus argument "collapses into cynicism."
The memo answer: no, and the revision must close this door firmly. To say
that consensus is manufactured closure is not to say the closed content is
false. It is to say that the announcement of consensus certifies a completed
risk redistribution, not a belief distribution — and that the two can move
independently. The impedance case gives both directions: consensus declared
over live disagreement, and durable consensus surviving evidential erosion
because reopening it would re-individualize blame. A cynic thinks the
machinery discredits the content. Our claim is narrower and more useful:
whoever wants to know what a field believes should not read its consensus
statements, and whoever wants to know what a field will defend should read
nothing else.

Reviewer B also wants the two concepts connected. The connection is risk,
and the revision should make it the spine of the discussion section.
Novelty, on our account, is the controlled tearing of expectations;
consensus is the controlled sealing of them. Both are budgeted activities.
Communities ration how much novelty they can afford to ratify, because each
repair scene consumes collective attention; they ration how much consensus
they can afford to declare, because each closure event obligates future
maintenance. A field's intellectual metabolism is visible in how it spends
these two budgets, and the pathological cases line up neatly: fields that
advertise novelty faster than audiences can repair it, and fields that
declare consensus faster than the machinery can carry the displaced risk.

Remaining tasks for the resubmission. Rewrite the abstract so that novelty
and consensus appear only in our register, not the inherited one — the
current draft slips twice, using novelty as a property of the pulse in the
narration and consensus as agreement in the survey paragraph. Add the seam
metaphor to the title candidates. And append the measurement note, since
both reviewers, in different keys, asked the same question: if novelty is
repair and consensus is closure, show us the ledger where you count them.
